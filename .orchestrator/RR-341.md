## Description

Fix a mounted continuity failure in which Relay Runner correctly detects a stalled foreground provider and launches the continuity agent, but cannot perform a recovery. The captured incident reached the broker, then rejected both `release_dead_ownership` and `launch_foreground_provider` with `target_health_not_proven`; the continuity agent exhausted its attempts and completed with `circuit_open` without restoring foreground processing or handing control back to the queued command.

Remove the circular recovery precondition without weakening the boundary around genuine live provider or manual turns. The broker must be able to act on privacy-safe, generation-scoped proof that one Relay-owned foreground provider is stalled, while an ambiguous or currently healthy target remains fail-closed. Keep the RR-332 through RR-336 privacy, duplicate-effect, project-mutation, and provider-parity guarantees intact.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A deterministic regression reproduces the mounted sequence: a `foreground_provider` incident is classified `stalled`, the continuity agent launches, health remains unproven, release and relaunch are rejected as `target_health_not_proven`, and the run ends `circuit_open` without a canonical handoff.
- [ ] The recovery broker can derive bounded authority from the exact incident, recovery generation, app-owned provider session, and stale ownership evidence needed to release or replace only the failed Relay-owned target.
- [ ] A genuine active manual turn, a healthy provider turn, an ambiguous session, a stale incident generation, or a cross-provider target cannot be released or relaunched by this path.
- [ ] A successful recovery records the component action, restored provider health for the required stable interval, canonical foreground handoff, and continued processing of the original queued command or an explicit repeat request.
- [ ] Recovery produces no duplicate command execution, duplicate TTS, stale reply, continuity-agent speech, unauthorized project mutation, or private transcript, prompt, repository, screenshot, credential, raw-log, or external-data disclosure.
- [ ] If safe recovery remains impossible, the circuit-open outcome becomes an explicit actionable user-visible state and persists the sanitized unresolved report and editable follow-up proposals required by the continuity design.
- [ ] Codex and Claude deterministic coverage exercises equivalent safety and recovery outcomes with only documented provider-specific health or launch signals.
- [ ] A signed mounted-app verification records one real recovery per provider, correlated command and recovery identities, terminal delivery, speech playback, and human audible attestation separately from source-test evidence.

## Live regression evidence

- The mounted app classified a Codex foreground-provider stall and launched the continuity agent.
- Health checks reported no restored objective; scoped release and relaunch attempts were rejected as `target_health_not_proven`.
- The continuity agent completed `circuit_open`, leaving recovery unsuccessful from the user's perspective.
