## Description

Give Relay Runner one coherent policy for a new voice turn that arrives while
the foreground Codex or Claude orchestrator is already working, and one
authoritative boundary for deciding what may be spoken.

Today every normal user utterance is delivered concurrently to the persistent
fast messenger and the foreground orchestrator. That parallel fan-out is
valuable: it lets the messenger respond naturally while the orchestrator
plans, uses tools, manages tickets, and coordinates workers. Relay Runner also
has one normal bridge-owned TTS execution queue. However, several semantic
producers and compatibility fallbacks can still contend before playback:
messenger handoffs, progress and clarification events, authoritative
orchestrator replies, completion-hook recovery, messenger-failure fallbacks,
manual bridge compatibility paths, and TTS controls. This can produce late,
duplicated, or out-of-order speech that feels like two agents talking over one
another even when the audio executor itself is serial.

The active-work side has a related ambiguity. A newer user message may be
additive, an independent bounded task, project-changing work that belongs on
the board, a request that conflicts with the current job, or an explicit
replacement/cancellation. Newest-intent and exactly-once delivery protect
against several stale-command races, but they do not yet provide one explicit
disposition for this relationship. The foreground orchestrator may therefore
queue, interrupt, or adopt the new work inconsistently, and an interruption
can bulldoze useful accepted work.

There is also a concrete pre-classification interrupt in the current input
path. Starting a real held-to-talk recording silences TTS and then publishes
`__INTERRUPT__` before the completed transcript is known. App-owned delivery
lets that control bypass normal busy-turn deferral and sends Ctrl-C to the
active provider. Relay Runner therefore interrupts foreground work before it
can determine whether the utterance is additive, independent, conflicting, or
an explicit redirect. Decouple conversational barge-in from work preemption:
recording start may stop/mute speech, but provider interruption requires a
completed intent that has been classified as cancel, redirect, or replace.

Implement the feature as two coordinated but separate layers:

1. **Work intent arbitration.** The foreground orchestrator remains the sole
   planning and mutation authority. For every substantive new turn, resolve
   one explicit disposition relative to accepted active work:
   `continue_current`, `run_sidecar`, `queue_project_work`,
   `clarify_priority`, `replace_current`, or `control_only`. Queueing is the
   safe default when work conflicts. Only an explicit stop, cancel, redirect,
   or replacement may preempt accepted work without clarification.
2. **Speech arbitration.** Add one bridge-local speech coordinator in front of
   the existing TTS worker. It is the only production component allowed to
   enqueue, replace, stop, skip, or replay speech. Messenger, orchestrator,
   lifecycle, completion, and fallback paths submit typed speech intents to
   this coordinator rather than writing playable text directly.

Back work arbitration with a durable ordered `IntentInbox` and an explicit
`IntentDisposition`; do not reinterpret the latest-only
`/tmp/voice_cmd_ready` mailbox as a queue. Each intent needs stable identity,
claim/ack state, relationship to active work, and authorization effect.
App-owned and manual bridge modes must consume the same logical inbox protocol
even if their provider-delivery transports remain different.

Keep work authorization and speech freshness distinct. An additive status or
inspection turn may become the newest conversation without revoking an older
command's bounded project mutation, while any no-longer-useful pending speech
for that older conversational moment may still be suppressed. Preserve the
bounded mutation authorization and exactly-once guarantees established by
RR-218 and RR-238.

### Work intent policy

- `continue_current`: the new turn adds context, asks for status, answers a
  clarification, or safely steers the active job. Apply it to the current
  orchestration context without creating a competing execution owner.
- `run_sidecar`: dispatch a self-contained, independently verifiable task only
  when it does not depend on the active job's unfinished result and does not
  contend for an exclusive resource. Suitable examples include multi-step
  read-only research or inspection. A one-call screenshot or similarly
  bounded read can remain inline. Project mutations continue to follow the
  Relay ticket/worktree contract rather than becoming unaudited sidecars.
- `queue_project_work`: create or refine the appropriate native Relay Runner
  ticket without silently replacing active work. Promotion into the Queued
  lane remains an explicit dispatch decision under the existing board
  lifecycle.
- `clarify_priority`: when both queueing and switching are plausible and the
  choice materially changes the result, ask the user once whether to queue or
  switch. Do not force this question for obvious additive or independent work.
- `replace_current`: revoke or cancel only the work actually superseded by a
  clear user redirect. Report any irreversible or already-started effects.
- `control_only`: handle play, stop, replay, interrupt, cancel, and other
  deliberate controls without manufacturing a project ticket.

The disposition record should carry at least an intent id, route, target or
conflicting work ids, a short public reason or clarification question, and the
effect on existing mutation authorization. A safe provisional route is queue;
no foreground Ctrl-C occurs merely because the user started recording.

Sidecars must declare and respect exclusive resource ownership. At minimum,
the same repository/working tree, the shared desktop UI, and external
side-effect channels cannot be driven concurrently by unrelated owners. A
sidecar reports structured lifecycle and outcome events back to the foreground
orchestrator; it never speaks directly and never becomes a second authority.

### Speech intent contract

Define one provider-neutral speech-intent envelope carrying enough metadata to
arbitrate without another model call. It should include:

- Relay command sequence and command id;
- a stable response/utterance id and deduplication key;
- source (`messenger`, `orchestrator`, `lifecycle`, `completion`, or
  `fallback`);
- semantic kind (`handoff`, `progress`, `clarification`, `final`, `fallback`,
  or `control`);
- authoritative/non-authoritative status and priority;
- display text separately from a semantic spoken brief or rendered spoken
  text;
- creation time, optional expiry/deadline, and replacement policy; and
- the resolved work disposition when it is relevant to the user-facing reply.

The orchestrator supplies truthful public state and a compact semantic brief.
The persistent messenger remains tool-free and turns that brief, the actual
user utterance, and recent safe public context into concise natural language.
Production speech must not select a fixed sentence or deterministic phrase for
routine queue, sidecar, or switch outcomes. Tests should assert semantic
invariants and state truthfulness rather than exact wording. A last-resort
failure path may speak the substantive authoritative answer directly, but it
must still pass through the speech coordinator and must not introduce a
repetitive content-free acknowledgement.

The coordinator revalidates an event atomically when it is accepted, after any
messenger rendering, and immediately before playback starts. The TTS worker
becomes a single-flight executor for committed speech plans and reports
`queued`, `started`, `completed`, `cancelled`, or `failed`; producer paths do
not call its input queue directly.

### Ordering, freshness, and interruption

- Continue sending each user turn to messenger and orchestrator concurrently.
  The arbiter is a local gate, not an additional model round-trip or fixed
  debounce.
- Accept a fast messenger handoff immediately when its command is current and
  no authoritative result has already won. If a final arrives before that
  handoff starts, suppress the handoff. If the handoff is already playing,
  sequence a still-useful final after it instead of overlapping or restarting
  the same thought.
- A same-command progress event cannot preempt a handoff already being
  generated or spoken. Clarifications and finals supersede obsolete pending
  progress. Deduplicate explicit orchestrator replies, completion-hook
  recoveries, and messenger/fallback renderings of the same outcome.
- A genuinely newer user turn invalidates stale pending speech using Relay
  sequence/id currentness. Recording barge-in stops current playback without
  sending provider Ctrl-C. The completed turn then competes normally, and only
  a classified cancel/redirect/replace may interrupt active work. Stopping
  speech must not cancel already accepted background work.
- Replay operates on the last accepted replayable speech intent and cannot
  resurrect a stale producer path or duplicate a completion.
- Exactly one synthesis/playback process may be active. Chimes, play, replay,
  and higher-priority replacements participate in the same serialization;
  replacement waits for an observed cancellation before starting.
- Direct FIFO/TTS writes outside the coordinator are removed or confined to a
  clearly documented bootstrap/emergency boundary that still enforces
  currentness and deduplication.

### Staged implementation

1. Introduce the speech-intent type and coordinator at the existing
   `voice_bridge.py` to `tts_worker.py` boundary. Route every current producer
   through it in behavior-preserving/shadow mode, add source diagnostics, and
   retain the current fast parallel fan-out.
2. Enable sequence/id freshness, same-command ordering, replacement,
   deduplication, single-flight chime/play/replay, and speech-only barge-in
   rules. Remove direct compatibility writes after equivalent behavior is
   covered.
3. Add the durable intent inbox, foreground work-disposition contract, and
   resource-aware sidecar lane. Feed public disposition outcomes to the
   messenger for natural realization, classify before provider interruption,
   and keep project mutations on the native ticket/worktree path.
4. Validate Codex, Claude, app-owned sessions, manual `relay-bridge`, messenger
   failure, provider completion failure, and installed-app behavior before
   retiring legacy fallbacks.

Preserve the response-preview separation delivered by RR-239: authoritative
display text and spoken rendering may differ, but acknowledgements and progress
cannot overwrite the final visual response. Preserve the natural contextual
handoff behavior delivered by RR-166 and the messenger authority boundary
introduced by RR-161.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every substantive voice turn resolves to exactly one documented work
      disposition: continue, sidecar, queue, clarify, replace, or control.
- [ ] An ordered durable intent inbox preserves more than the latest pending
      command, assigns stable intent/claim/ack identity, and is shared
      logically by app-owned and manual bridge delivery. It does not overload
      `/tmp/voice_cmd_ready` as a queue.
- [ ] A new turn does not preempt accepted work by default. Only an explicit
      cancel/replace/redirect or a user choice after material ambiguity revokes
      the affected work.
- [ ] Starting or completing an ordinary recording can stop/mute speech
      without sending Ctrl-C to Codex or Claude. Provider interruption occurs
      only after the completed turn is classified as explicit cancel,
      redirect, or replace.
- [ ] Additive, status, inspection, and clarification turns can be handled
      immediately without revoking older bounded project mutations.
- [ ] Sidecar eligibility is enforced and tested: work is independent,
      bounded, verifiable, and free of conflicting repository, desktop, or
      external-side-effect ownership. Sidecars cannot speak or perform
      unaudited project mutations.
- [ ] Project-changing requests continue to use native Relay tickets and
      isolated worktrees; the new sidecar path does not bypass the command
      action contract.
- [ ] The orchestrator provides semantic state/disposition briefs and the
      messenger renders them naturally from current context. Production code
      does not choose a canned queue/switch/sidecar sentence, and tests assert
      meaning rather than exact prose.
- [ ] One speech coordinator is the exclusive production gateway for
      messenger, orchestrator, lifecycle, completion, fallback, replay, stop,
      and skip requests before they reach the existing TTS executor.
- [ ] Speech eligibility is revalidated atomically at acceptance, after
      messenger rendering, and immediately before playback; sequence/id and
      work-scope metadata survive until playback completion.
- [ ] The speech-intent envelope carries command sequence/id, response
      identity, source, semantic kind, authority, display/spoken semantics,
      freshness, deduplication, and replacement metadata.
- [ ] Explicit orchestrator replies, provider completion hooks, messenger
      finals, and failure fallbacks can produce at most one accepted spoken
      final and one stable authoritative preview for a command.
- [ ] Same-command handoff, progress, clarification, and final events obey the
      specified ordering. They cannot overlap, silently bulldoze one another,
      or leave obsolete queued speech behind.
- [ ] A newer command or barge-in stops/suppresses stale speech promptly while
      preserving accepted background work unless work cancellation was also
      authorized.
- [ ] Replay speaks the last accepted replayable result once and cannot revive
      a stale acknowledgement, progress event, or completion fallback.
- [ ] Fake-player concurrency tests prove a maximum of one active playback
      process across speech, chime, play-while-playing, replay-while-playing,
      stop, and higher-priority replacement. Stop/cancel acknowledgement is
      observed before replacement starts.
- [ ] User turns still fan out to messenger and orchestrator concurrently.
      Arbitration adds no model round-trip or fixed debounce; a deterministic
      bridge benchmark keeps coordinator decision latency below 10 ms p95 and
      adds no more than 50 ms p95 to the existing time-to-TTS-enqueue path.
- [ ] Instrumentation distinguishes proposed, accepted, replaced, expired,
      deduplicated, interrupted, played, and replayed intents without logging
      raw private transcripts, hidden reasoning, terminal input, or secrets.
- [ ] Codex and Claude app-owned sessions satisfy the same work-disposition,
      speech-ordering, stale-output, failure-recovery, and latency contract.
      Provider-specific turn and completion mechanics are documented and
      covered, including equivalent Codex Stop failure recovery and Claude
      StopFailure handling.
- [ ] Manual `relay-bridge`, `__INTERRUPT__`, TTS stop/play/replay gestures,
      messenger-disabled/failure operation, completion-hook recovery, and
      response-preview behavior remain functional.
- [ ] Deterministic race tests cover messenger-first, orchestrator-first,
      same-command progress during handoff, late completion, duplicate final,
      the current skip-before-state window, rapid queued turns, newer-turn
      barge-in, sidecar completion during another turn, and explicit replace
      versus additive status.
- [ ] Relevant focused Python and Swift suites, generated-instruction checks,
      full tests, and an installed-app voice smoke pass.

## Out of scope

- Replacing the current STT, messenger, or TTS models.
- Giving the messenger tools, planning authority, or repository access.
- Running two audio streams concurrently.
- Redesigning Workspace board columns or dispatch lifecycle.
- Allowing ticketless sidecars to mutate project code.
