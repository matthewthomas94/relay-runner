## Description

Spec: `docs/specs/relay-remote.md` (Relay Remote: iOS voice over SSH), committed as `2a64cc0d` on branch `claude/ios-relay-ssh-tcp-spec-ddb8f2`. The spec is not on `main` yet; if the file is missing from your worktree, read it with `git show 2a64cc0d:docs/specs/relay-remote.md`. Read these sections first: iOS app, Provider parity, Verified upstream inputs, Open questions.

The iOS app keeps speech on the phone: on-device speech-to-text produces `utterance` frames, and on-device text-to-speech speaks the `speech` frames the Mac sends back; only text crosses the wire. The spec names two candidate stacks and does not pick one.

Compare them with public evidence pinned to a commit SHA or a dated document:

- FluidAudio on iOS: the STT models (Parakeet TDT v3, Parakeet EOU for end-of-utterance, Phonon-2), the TTS models (Kokoro, PocketTTS), minimum iOS version, on-device model sizes and download or caching behaviour, and what the project states about validation on iPhone hardware as opposed to macOS. The Mac app pins FluidAudio by revision in `Package.swift` (line 8); say whether that revision supports iOS.
- Apple frameworks: `SpeechAnalyzer` and `SpeechTranscriber` (iOS 26) and the on-device mode of `SFSpeechRecognizer` (iOS 18) for STT; `AVSpeechSynthesizer` for TTS; voice quality, latency, end-of-utterance behaviour, and language coverage.

Recommend the first-ship STT engine, TTS engine, and the resulting iOS deployment target (iOS 18 or iOS 26). Name the parity gaps between the phone path and the Mac path (the Mac uses FluidAudio for STT and Kokoro for TTS): different voices, different end-of-utterance behaviour, anything the phone cannot do.

This is read-only research: do not build a prototype. Engine integration is RR-429.
