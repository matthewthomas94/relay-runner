## Description

Make the existing recording and playback dot-matrix glyphs respond to live audio energy so their motion reads like a restrained pseudo waveform. During recording, animate from the user's microphone signal. During response playback and replay, animate from the speech audio being played. Silence should settle toward a calm baseline and stronger speech should produce visibly greater movement without becoming noisy or visually unrelated to the audio.

Preserve the current visual design. Keep the existing 24-point artwork bounds, twelve-dot arrangement, dot sizes, white/accent color assignments, orange Listening treatment, blue Playing treatment, notch placement, and surrounding overlay geometry. Change only the motion behavior needed to make the dots audio-reactive; do not redesign the glyph, add a conventional waveform, change the notch layout, or alter Working and Not working artwork.

Derive a normalized, privacy-preserving level envelope from the in-process recording and playback pipelines. Downsample and smooth it before UI delivery so audio callbacks remain real-time safe and the main thread is not flooded. The visual mapping must be deterministic, bounded, and resilient to silence, clipping, device changes, cancellation, playback failure, replay, and rapid recording/playback transitions. Do not persist raw samples or level history and do not send audio telemetry outside the existing audio path.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Recording dot motion responds to the live microphone amplitude envelope, with a calm near-silence state and visibly stronger but bounded motion during speech.
- [ ] Initial playback and explicit replay respond to the amplitude envelope of the speech audio actually being rendered, rather than using an unrelated looping animation.
- [ ] The existing twelve-dot geometry, 24-point bounds, dot diameters, white/accent assignments, orange Listening palette, blue Playing palette, notch placement, and surrounding overlay design remain unchanged.
- [ ] The pseudo-waveform mapping changes dot position and/or opacity only within documented safe bounds, preserves legibility at peaks, and never clips outside the existing artwork footprint.
- [ ] Audio level extraction is allocation-safe and nonblocking on real-time callbacks; UI updates are rate-limited, delivered safely to the main actor, and smoothed with documented attack/release behavior so speech feels responsive without jitter.
- [ ] Silence, clipping, unavailable levels, device switches, capture restart, playback preparation, stop, cancel, failure, replay completion, and state interruption reset or settle the animation without stale motion leaking into another state.
- [ ] Reduce Motion uses a static or materially reduced-motion Listening/Playing presentation while retaining the current status colors and clear state distinction.
- [ ] Working and Not working motion, foreground/background playback ownership, media pause/resume, recording delivery, TTS audio, replay eligibility, and cancellation behavior are unchanged.
- [ ] No raw audio samples, per-frame level history, transcripts, or derived voice biometrics are persisted, logged, or transmitted for the animation.
- [ ] Deterministic tests cover silence, representative speech envelopes, peaks/clamping, smoothing, reset behavior, Reduce Motion, recording/playback state changes, and unchanged glyph geometry/colors.
- [ ] Focused audio and notch tests pass, and a fresh built app completes mounted microphone-speech plus TTS-playback/replay checks showing visible audio correlation without capture glitches, playback underruns, or layout changes.
- [ ] The run log records the signal source for recording and playback, update frequency, smoothing and bounds, measured audio/UI impact, automated checks, and the mounted verification performed.
