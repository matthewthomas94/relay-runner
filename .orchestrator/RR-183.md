### First-run ownership

The dedicated full-screen intro presentation must own the entire fresh-install permission sequence. Do not hand off to the Settings-hosted onboarding view after `I need a few permissions /`. Continue on the intro-owned Workspace surface with the four missing permissions in `PermissionKind.guidedSetupOrder`: Microphone, Accessibility, Input Monitoring, then Screen Recording. Already-granted permissions skip automatically. Keep the Figma prompt copy and `Grant permission` treatment from RR-182, including the supplied Accessibility extension.

The existing `PermissionSetupCoordinator` remains the authority for native prompts, System Settings routing, polling, companion lifecycle, and grant notifications. Integrate the intro presentation with that coordinator rather than duplicating permission APIs. Microphone invokes Apple's prompt from the intro. Settings-backed permissions fully retract the intro surface before System Settings and the drag companion take focus. Once a grant is observed, close the companion and reveal the intro again at the next missing permission. A denial or closed Settings window returns to the same intro permission prompt with a working retry path.

Only after the initial permission sequence has been handled should the non-permission setup flow hand off to the existing Settings-hosted onboarding content for provider/model/effort/workspace, Python bootstrap, Codex or Claude authentication, Ready, and Start Session. The Settings-hosted `OnboardingView` must not render first-run hero permission prompts. The normal `StatusSettingsTab` privacy rows remain the ongoing place to inspect, grant, restore, or revoke permissions after onboarding; granted rows must continue to look and behave as they do today. A returning onboarded user whose permission is later revoked should use those normal Settings management rows rather than being forced back through a first-run hero prompt on launch.

Fresh-launch interruption and Reduce Motion must obey the same ownership boundary. Relaunching during the initial permission sequence resumes on the intro surface at the first missing permission, not in Settings. Reduce Motion may skip the cinematic typing and particle travel, but it must show a static intro-owned permission prompt and must never route initial permission requests into Settings-hosted onboarding. Generic cinematic click/key skip may advance to the first permission prompt, but must not bypass or dismiss an interactive permission decision.

### Exact shared particle renderer

Delete the bespoke `OnboardingHalftoneFieldLayout`/manual dot drawing path introduced by RR-182. The onboarding intro must instantiate and reuse the same `ParticleFieldRenderer` implementation and `.tts` theme used by `OverlayController` for `OverlayState.speaking`/message playing. Do not create a lookalike, copy its grid math, or maintain a second palette/wave implementation. The shared renderer must remain clipped to the intro Workspace surface and be laid out correctly across displays and backing scales. If a small reusable host/lifecycle adapter is needed, share that adapter without changing the visual output of the production listening/playing overlay.

Figma motion inspection for section `1029:1987` returned an empty animated-node inventory (`{"nodes":[]}`), so no authoritative Figma timing values exist. The slower pacing below is an explicit product adjustment from the user's feedback, not a claimed Figma export.

### Slower readable pacing

Retain the annotated cursor behavior and phrase order, but slow the cinematic to approximately twice its current pace. Target a total timeline around 9.5–11.5 seconds, excluding reveal/dismiss transitions, with:

- typing around 0.060–0.070 seconds per grapheme;
- erasing around 0.040–0.050 seconds per grapheme;
- an initial brand hold of at least 0.65 seconds;
- the message-playing particle reveal/travel held for roughly 1.8–2.2 seconds;
- intermediate phrase holds of at least 1.0 second;
- a final `I need a few permissions /` hold of at least 1.4 seconds before the first permission prompt.

Keep the timeline display-refresh driven, preserve the blinking `/`, erase every previous phrase grapheme-by-grapheme, and keep text centered without horizontal jumping. Do not insert arbitrary sleeps into UI code; express pacing as deterministic timeline constants covered by tests.

The screenshot references show desktop wallpaper below the 736-point Workspace surface; preserve that existing reveal geometry. Do not regress RR-179/RR-181: the draggable app companion must still provide a real Finder-compatible drag, smooth hover continuity, hand-above-card layering, click-through demo, and no green drop ring.
